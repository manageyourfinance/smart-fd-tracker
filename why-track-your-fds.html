Why every investor should track their FDs

By Chaitanya Mankar · Updated 15 October 2026 · 5 min read

A fixed deposit looks like the simplest investment there is: put money in, collect it back with interest. In practice, most families end up with FDs spread across banks, names and years. That is where the trouble starts.

What goes wrong when FDs are not tracked

  • A maturity passes unnoticed. Many banks renew an FD automatically at the rate in force that day, which may be lower than what you could have chosen. If you did not choose renewal, the money may sit in a savings account earning far less.
  • TDS arrives as a surprise. Banks deduct tax at source once your interest crosses a limit. If you did not expect it, your cash flow and your tax planning are both off.
  • Form 121 is missed. If your total tax is nil, a declaration lets the bank skip TDS, but it must reach each bank, before the interest is credited, every year. With four banks, that is four dates to remember. See Form 15G vs Form 15H explained.
  • Advance tax is forgotten. FD interest is income. If your tax after TDS is large enough, advance tax applies. See Advance tax for FD interest.
  • Too much sits with one bank. Deposit insurance covers up to ₹5 lakh per depositor per bank, principal and interest together. Only a full list shows how much you hold where.

Interest is taxed every year, not at maturity

For a cumulative FD, interest builds up each year, and it is generally taxed in the year it accrues, even though you receive the money only at maturity. A five-year FD therefore adds to your taxable income in each of those five years. Tracking the yearly interest per profile tells you what to expect at filing time.

What to keep track of

  • Bank, FD number and the holder’s name (and whether it is joint)
  • Amount, interest rate, start date and maturity date
  • Cumulative or payout, and how often interest is paid out
  • Whether TDS is deducted, and whether the holder is a senior citizen
  • Nominee and renewal instructions

Spreadsheet or an FD tracker app?

A spreadsheet works for a few FDs, if you keep it up to date and build your own formulas. It does not remind you, and it does not know that interest compounds every quarter or how TDS applies to each family member.

A simple routine

  1. Enter every FD once, including old ones.
  2. Before each April, check which banks need a fresh Form 121.
  3. A month before a maturity, check current rates and decide: renew, withdraw or move.
  4. Each quarter, glance at the interest earned to date.
