How FD interest is calculated

By Chaitanya Mankar · Updated 15 October 2026 · 6 min read

Every bank shows an FD rate, but the amount you receive depends on how the interest is added. Here is how it works, so you can check any FD yourself or use an FD maturity calculator.

Cumulative FD: compound interest

In a cumulative FD, interest is added to your deposit and earns interest too. Most Indian banks compound every quarter. The formula is:

Maturity amount = P × (1 + r ÷ 4) ^ (4 × t)

P is the amount you deposit, r is the yearly rate as a decimal (7% is 0.07) and t is the tenure in years.

Worked example

₹1,00,000 at 7% a year for 3 years, compounded quarterly:

StepValue
Quarterly rate7% ÷ 4 = 1.75%
Number of quarters3 × 4 = 12
Maturity amount₹1,00,000 × (1.0175)12 ≈ ₹1,23,144
Interest earned≈ ₹23,144

With simple interest, the same FD would earn ₹21,000. The extra ₹2,144 is the effect of compounding.

Stated rate and effective yield

A 7% FD compounded quarterly really grows by about 7.19% in a year. Compare FDs by effective yield when their payout or compounding patterns differ.

Payout FDs: interest paid out

In a non-cumulative FD, interest is paid to you regularly (monthly, quarterly, half-yearly or yearly) and your deposit stays the same. The interest for each period is a share of the yearly interest:

  • Monthly payout on ₹5,00,000 at 7%: about ₹2,917
  • Quarterly payout: about ₹8,750

Some banks work out monthly and quarterly payouts on a slightly discounted basis, so your actual payout can be a little lower. Your bank’s schedule is the final word.

Things that change the result

  • Senior citizen rate. Banks usually pay senior citizens an extra 0.25% to 0.50% on FDs. Check each bank’s current senior citizen FD rates before you choose.
  • Tenure in days. Short FDs are priced by days, and the bank’s own day count applies.
  • Premature withdrawal. Banks usually charge a penalty (often 0.5% to 1% lower rate) if you close early.
  • TDS. The bank may deduct tax from the interest, so what reaches you can be lower. See How TDS on FD works.
