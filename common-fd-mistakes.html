Common FD mistakes to avoid

By Chaitanya Mankar · Updated 15 October 2026 · 5 min read

Most FD mistakes are not about choosing the wrong bank. They come from small things that nobody was watching. Here are eight, with the fix for each. Good fixed deposit management is mostly about not letting these happen.

1. Letting an FD renew without a decision

Auto-renewal is convenient, but the new rate is whatever the bank offers that day. Check rates a few weeks before maturity, then renew, withdraw or move the money deliberately.

2. Forgetting Form 121 every April

If your tax is nil, a Form 121 stops TDS, but only if the bank has it before the interest is credited, each year and for each bank. Set a reminder for April.

3. Ignoring TDS and the tax return

TDS is only an advance payment. FD interest still has to be part of your return, and it should match the AIS. See How TDS on FD works.

4. Skipping advance tax

If your tax after TDS is ₹10,000 or more, advance tax may apply, unless you are a senior citizen with no business income. Late payment can bring interest. See Advance tax for FD interest.

5. Keeping more than ₹5 lakh in one bank

Deposit insurance covers up to ₹5 lakh per depositor per bank, for principal and interest together. This is a limit to track, especially in joint and family FDs.

6. Comparing only the headline rate

A cumulative FD and a monthly payout FD at the same rate do not give the same return. Compare effective yields and think about whether you want income or growth. See How FD interest is calculated.

7. Missing the senior citizen rate

Banks usually pay senior citizens extra on FDs, and the TDS limit is also higher. Mark the holder as a senior citizen, and compare senior citizen FD rates across banks, not just at your own.

8. Breaking an FD without checking the penalty

Closing early usually means a lower rate for the period the money stayed, plus a possible penalty. For a short need, check whether a loan against the FD costs less.
