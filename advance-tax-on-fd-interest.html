Advance tax for FD interest

By Chaitanya Mankar · Updated 15 October 2026 · 6 min read

TDS may not cover all the tax on your FD interest. If a balance is left, you may need to pay it in instalments during the year, which is called advance tax.

Who has to pay advance tax?

  • If the tax on your estimated income for the year, after TDS, is ₹10,000 or more, advance tax applies.
  • Resident senior citizens (60 and above) with no business or professional income do not have to pay advance tax. They pay any balance when they file their return.

How to estimate it

  1. Add up your expected income: FD interest for the full year (including interest that accrues but is paid at maturity), salary, rent and others.
  2. Work out the tax at your slab rate for your regime.
  3. Subtract the TDS already deducted or expected, including TDS on your FD interest.
  4. If what remains is ₹10,000 or more, split it by the schedule above.

Worked example

Your estimated tax on all income is ₹52,000. TDS on your FD interest and other income is ₹30,000. Tax left to pay: ₹22,000. This is above ₹10,000, so advance tax applies.

What if you pay late or short?

Interest is charged on the shortfall, at 1% a month, for each instalment that falls short. If you notice it late, you can pay the balance as self-assessment tax before filing, but the interest for the earlier delay still applies. It is better to pay on time.

Where FD interest fits in

For a cumulative FD, interest accrues every year, so each year’s accrued interest counts, even if the bank pays it only at maturity. Banks deduct TDS on it, so TDS covers part of the tax, and you may owe the rest. See How TDS on FD works.
